# LAW_V 504A A99_B 2025W1 Property Law

This Distance Learning online course runs September 8th through December 22nd, 2026, including the examination period.

This course introduces the conceptual foundations of property, the basic principles of property law, and the legal regime that regulates land ownership and its transfer in British Columbia. In doing so, it provides a foundation for many courses in the upper year law school curriculum. It also provides a framework in which to understand how the common law works and to consider many fundamental questions about the common law and its role as a social institution.

## Author

This course was written and created by Professor **Douglas Harris**, the Nathan T. Nemetz Chair in Legal History at UBC’s Allard School of Law and an award-winning scholar and teacher. He has taught the introductory property course at UBC since 2001. His particular areas of doctrinal expertise include Aboriginal rights and title, land title registration systems, and condominium law. He is one of the co-authors of the casebook for this course: A Property Law Reader: Cases, Questions, and Commentary. ## Structure & Content

LAW 504 Property Law is a 5-credit course that runs over two terms when taught in the classroom to first year JD students. This distance-learning version is condensed into a single term. If it were taught in a classroom, **there would be 10 hours of in-class instruction each week plus readings and exercises in preparation for, and to be completed after class**. This provides some sense of the amount of time you will need to allot each week in order to successfully complete this course.

The course is divided into 12 Units (see below). Each Unit is then divided into several Topics, and each is organized the same way. The Units begin with a short Unit Overview, including a list of the Topics. This is followed by description of the expected Unit Outcomes in point form.

Each Topic begins with an introduction to some of the important ideas and principal legal concepts. This is followed by a discussion and description of an **Activity**. It is here that you will find direction about the required readings, problems, videos, supplemental material, etc.

Each Unit includes a **Unit Review Quiz**. **These are self-tests; they do not form part of your final grade.** The questions are not difficult, and they are not the type of questions that you will have on the examinations. Instead, they are intended to point you to some of the important concepts in the Units. You will have access to the answers once you have completed each quiz.

At the end of each unit there is a list of cases, statutes and additional materials referred to in that unit. Some, but not all of the items listed under Additional Material are required. You will find direction about what is required in the Activity sections and in the Course Syllabus.

## Course Expectations

### Questions and Discussion

It is expected that students will answer the questions posed in the course material and **contribute to the online discussion**. 

Through each unit there are sections marked "**Activity**".   Each of these Activity sections will ask you to consult a certain resource and consider some questions related to the content that you have read.  **These Activity sections should guide your participation in the discussion forum.**  It is expected that **students will reply to at least 2 Activity prompts for each Unit (ie. 2 posts per week)**.  You may reply to more prompts if you would like.  **Contribution includes not only posting your answer but interacting with your classmates through discussion as well.** 

Note that Academic Integrity rules apply to your discussion posts.  All posts in the discussion forum should either be your own original work or properly cited where you are relying or quoting the works of others.  If you are unclear about the rules regarding Academic Integrity please speak with your instructor.

All discussions are monitored by the instructor and the quality of your contributions will be reflected in your participation grade.  The instructor will not post on every thread and will only get involved in online discussions from time to time if there is a need to clarify a legal point being discussed or if the discussion thread requires intervention for some other reason.  The instructor is always avaliable to answer any questions or provide clarification on any course related topic through the Q & A discussion forum or through direct e-mail contact.

### Evaluation

The evaluation for this course consists of three elements:

- Participation: 20%

- Mid Term Examination: 20%

- Final Examination: 60%

The Mid Term examination will take place following Unit 6.  **The Mid Term will be a take home examination**.  The exam will be distributed and collected through the Canvas Platform.  You will have **48 hours** to complete and submit the exam.

The Final Examination will take place during the regular Allard School of Law examination period.  The Final Examination will be conducted through Proctorio.

## Course Reading Materials

The course materials include the course web site, a required casebook, a recommended textbook, and a range of supplemental material. The Activity section in each topic will direct you to the readings in the books and the supplemental material. All the supplemental material is available electronically.

The required casebook is:

- Harris, Douglas, et al., ***A Property Law Reader: Cases, Questions and Commentary, 5th Edition***. Carswell, 2022. ISBN: 9781668714492

N.B. the ***Property Law reader*** is available at 50% of the original list price at the UBC Bookstore. Please find the reader [linked](https://bookstore.ubc.ca/products/property-law-reader-cases-questions-commentary-5-stpv?variant=51689970204946&_pos=1&_sid=d6f74ff44&_ss=r) here.

As a casebook, this is a compilation of excerpts from cases, academic articles, law commission reports, and other sources, with some commentary and questions interspersed throughout. Before your beginning the units, read the Preface in the Reader, iii-v.

The recommended textbook is:

- Eran Kaplinsky, Malcolm Lavoie, and Jane Thomson, Ziff’s [*Principles of Property Law*](https://store.thomsonreuters.ca/en-ca/products/ziffs-principles-of-property-law-eighth-edition-softbound-book-43164269), 8th Ed. (Toronto: Carswell, 2023)

This textbook provides a comprehensive exposition of the fundamentals of Canadian property law. It includes many references to cases and other sources, but not excerpts from the cases themselves. Although **it is not required reading**, the chapters in the textbook parallel those of the casebook so if you are ever confused or uncertain, it is an excellent resource.
