# The Nature of Property



!!! note "From Kim"

    For Unit 2, you should be well versed in each of the following topics:

    **Sources of Property Law**

    - we will focus on aboriginal title in Unit 8, but for Unit 2 you should familarize yourself with first nations concepts of property law
    - understand the doctrine of tenture and its place in Canadian property law (what is relevant today?)
    - understand the doctrine of reception and generally how Canadian property law diverges from UK property law

    **Property, Class and Poverty (this is an area full of policy considerations, and is well placed for an essay on an exam - hint, hint)**

    - understand the connections with property law and homelessness
    - consider how Covid-19 has amplified these concerns re poverty and property - is there an answer or guidance in property law cases and theory?
    - you may also want to look at Prince George (City) v Stewart (Links to an external site.), 2021 BCSC 2089, a decision from the BC Supreme Court involving a tent encampment on municipal lands (this one in Prince George).

    **Protections for Property**

    - understand that property rights are not expressly protected in the Canadian constitution - why is this?  
    - understand the concepts and general statutory process for an expropriation in BC and a constructive expropriation (what is this?) - With regards to expropriation, you may be interested in the [article](https://www.canadaregulatoryreview.com/the-supreme-court-of-canada-clarifies-that-constructive-taking-does-not-require-actual-acquisition-of-property-by-the-state/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=the-supreme-court-of-canada-clarifies-that-constructive-taking-does-not-require-actual-acquisition-of-property-by-the-state&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=Daily%20Canada%20Regulatory%20Review&utm_content=Daily%20Canada%20Regulatory%20Review+CID_aa16a299d3c2e4714830b47150de64f1&utm_source=Campaign%20Monitor&utm_term=The%20Supreme%20Court%20of%20Canada%20clarifies%20that%20constructive%20taking%20does%20not%20require%20actual%20acquisition%20of%20property%20by%20the%20state){:target=" \_blank"} below summarizing the recent decision ***Annapolis Group Inc. v. Halifax Regional Municipality***, [2022 SCC 36](https://canlii.ca/t/jshfv){:target=" \_blank"} (***Annapolis v Halifax***), in which a 5-4 majority of the Supreme Court of Canada clarified that a *de facto* taking, or, as the majority preferred to call it, a “constructive taking”, of private property by the state does not require actual acquisition of a proprietary interest. Instead, the inquiry focuses on whether the state has, in effect, obtained an “advantage” from the property.
