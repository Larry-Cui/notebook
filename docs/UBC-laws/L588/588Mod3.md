## 3.1: Statutory and Code Provisions

Prior to examining the concepts and principles of advertising in the legal profession we will examine the relevant *Legal Profession Act* and B.C. **Code** provisions.  These statutory and codes sections govern your relationship as a lawyer to your client in relation to **client solicitation, legal fees and ethics**. 

**Fees & Disbursements**

The key sections of the *Legal Profession Act* and B.C. **Code** Rules to know in the area of client fees and agreements are:

- Sections **64 & 65**, which **define agreements** for legal services, contingency fees, bill, charges and other related terms.
- Sections **66 & 67**, which specifically outline the requirements pertaining to **Contingency Fee Agreements and related restrictions**.  
    - BC **Code** Rule 3.6 elaborates on fees disbursements.  They state that a lawyer who enters into a **contingent** fee agreement with a client must ensure that, under the circumstances existing at the time the agreement is entered into is **fair and reasonable**.  
    - Contingency fee agreements have to be **in writing** and there other rules relating to their form.  
    - Note that there are also **maximum amounts for contingency fee agreements for personal injury actions**. 
    - Rule 3.6 also outlines the requirements pertaining to **fee sharing agreements among lawyers**.  Fee sharing agreements occur when a lawyer meets with a client but then believes that the client would be better served by another lawyer. This will typically occur when the lawyer learns more about the client's case and discovers that it may be more complex or enters an area of law with which they do not have sufficient experience or specialization.  Fee Sharing arrangements are **fine as long as the ethics Rules 3.6-5 to 3.6-7 are followed**.  The rules mainly deal with restrictions on referrals to other lawyers and how fees may be split with another lawyer to avoid conflicts of interest.  
    - They ==**prohibit**== lawyers from entering into arrangements to **compensate or reward non-lawyers for the referral of clients**. It does ==**not prevent**== a lawyer from engaging in **promotional activities involving reasonable expenditures** on promotional items or activities that might result in the referral of clients generally by a non-lawyer.
- Section **68** allows for a client to apply for a hearing to the registrar of the court for **a review of a lawyer’s bill to a client as well as an examination of a retainer agreement**. If there is disagreement regarding the amount of a bill, **both lawyers and clients have the right** to have a lawyer’s bill reviewed by a BC Supreme Court Registrar, under Section 70 of the Act. (This is also referred to as **taxation of lawyers bills**”). **Only final bills may be reviewed** and a client may retain another lawyer to represent them in a hearing before the registrar.

**Competency and Quality of Service to Clients**

In terms of competency and quality of service in lawyer dealings for clients, the specific requirements are outlined in the B.C. **Code** in Rules 3.1 & 3.2.

**Rule 3.1 specifically addresses the competency of lawyers.** The provisions define competence using mandatory language:

> a lawyer must perform all legal services undertaken on a client’s behalf to the standard of a competent lawyer.  

This means being “**knowledgeable, skilled and capable**” in the practice of law. The reason for this provision is to reassure the public in general that the legal profession displays general competence, and the lawyer’s client in particular is entitled to assume that the lawyer has the ability and capacity to deal adequately with all of the client’s specific legal matters.  The concept of competence is based on principles of ethics and law, but this rule addresses the ethical principles. 

**Competence** involves **more than an understanding of legal principles**: it involves **an adequate knowledge of the practice and procedures** by which such principles can be ethically and effectively applied. Part of this for responsibility for lawyers involves **keeping up to date and knowledgeable about any changes** in areas of law in which you practise.

When we refer here the standard of the “**reasonably competent**” lawyer we are examining the **ethical considerations and ==not== liability for negligence**.  For ethics,  some of the factors that are analyzed here are more specific and flexible in terms of how lawyers should act on behalf of their clients.  The commentaries to this Rule note that there are many factors looked at to see if lawyers have employed the required skill or knowledge on behalf of their client, including:

- the complexity and specialized nature of the matter;
- the lawyer’s general experience;
- the lawyer’s training and experience in the field;
- the preparation and study the lawyer is able to give the matter; and
- whether it is appropriate or feasible to refer the matter to, or associate or consult with, a lawyer of established competence in the field in question.

**Rule 3.2 specifically outlines the requirements pertaining to quality of service provided to a client.**  Quality of service is related to acting as a competent lawyer.  Some of the examples given in the commentaries should be **fairly obvious matters** such as:

- keeping a client **reasonably informed** on their file;
- **responding** to a client’s telephone calls;
- **keeping appointments** with a client, or providing an explanation and apologizing for missing a meeting;
- doing promised work in a **timely manner** for maximum value to a client and providing them with **complete and accurate information** about the matter they have entrusted to you
- informing a client about the **status of their file**, including making a report to them when the matter is concluded
- **not drinking alcohol or using drugs** excessively, especially if it interferes with the lawyer’s ability to provide competent representation


**Being Civil**

The **honesty and candour requirement** also require a lawyer to be very open with a client about the real prospects for their case and occasionally requires being firm with the client.  Your opinion as a lawyer is the professional opinion.  The general principle is be **civil: firm but not rude**.

This rule also calls for out of court dispute resolution whenever possible (e.g. ADR, Mediation, etc.)  **Small claims cases generally have built in structures for mediation and claims resolution.** 

Resolution of a claim cannot generally include threatening criminal proceedings for private law actions.  The classic example here is debt collection.  Debt collectors in Canada and the United States are also known as skip tracers and are notorious for these types of threats.  **A letter from a competent lawyer in a civil debt matter should not threaten a ==criminal== proceeding** unless it is explicitly permitted by statute in certain circumstances (e.g. fraud).

This rule also **prohibits dishonesty and fraud in assisting a client**.  The language here is “**knows or ought to know**” that their client’s actions assists or encourages any dishonesty crime or fraud.  This is obviously a serious concern with criminal activities like mortgage fraud or money laundering and lawyers often legitimately provide services like purchasing or selling real estate or arranging financing for the purchase or sale of businesses or their assets. 

Finally, when a lawyer’s relationship to the client is in question because of the client's capacity (e.g. **diminished mental capacity** because of age or a medical condition) **an assessment is required** to demonstrate that they can actually understand any decisions that they are asking you to make on their behalf.  This assessment could involve a general perception of the person as having sufficient capacity to make decisions, or it could involve asking some probing questions about the matter to the person to reassure you that **there is sufficient capacity to allow you, as their lawyer, to act for them**.  Note that there is an ethical obligation to act for certain parties under some circumstances even where capacity is at issue (e.g. protection from self-harm) in order to protect a person until a legal representative can be appointed.  The Public Trustee or Guardian can also act as legal representative under some circumstances. 

## 3.2: Advertising

Ethical & Professional considerations relating to marketing and advertising of legal services in B.C. is governed by **Rule 4.2** and associated sub-rules of the **Code**.

- **Rule 4.2-3** notes that the section applies to **any “marketing activity”** undertaken or authorized by a lawyer in which they are identified as a lawyer, mediator or arbitrator.
- **Rule 4.2-4** defines “marketing activity” to **include any publication or communication** in the nature of an advertisement, promotional activity or material, letterhead, business card, listing in a directory, a public appearance or any other means by which professional legal services are promoted or clients are solicited.
- **Rule 4.2-5** outlines the **requirements for content and format** of marketing activities, stating that any marketing activity undertaken or authorized by a lawyer **must not be false, inaccurate, unverifiable, reasonably capable of misleading** the recipient/intended recipient or **contrary to the best interests of the public**.

Regarding the **formation of the lawyer-client relationship** there are certain ethical and professional requirements relevant to solicitation of clients.  Keep in mind the following key points:

- A formal legal relationship exists after a retainer agreement is signed but some **ethical obligations can arise even before retainer agreement is signed**.
- Tension often arises when **a client’s expectations** formed during the solicitation process do not meet up with the **practical obligations of the lawyer and the reality of a case**. Whether the lawyer charges more or less, and whatever they may promise, they are obligated to be a “reasonably competent lawyer.  They have to carry out this obligation within ethical and legal limits. Ethical issues sometimes arise for a lawyer when they feel pressured to go beyond ethical and possibly legal limits to fulfill a client’s expectations.
